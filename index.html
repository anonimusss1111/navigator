Mission rules and events

Planner mode

Verdict is a planning heuristic: Strong when Sun and Earth link are both available for at least 60% of the mission, Workable from 30%, otherwise Poor. A darkness limit overrides it.

Sunrise, sunset, Earthrise, Earthset

Why the Moon's south pole is hard

Elevation
Angle above the horizon. 0° is the horizon, 90° is straight up.
Azimuth
Compass direction along the horizon. 0° north, 90° east.
Libration
The slow rocking of the Moon that lets us see slightly past its edges. It moves Earth up and down near the horizon.
Earthset
The moment Earth drops below the horizon and a direct radio link to it ends.

Why LunarLens is different

Specialized for mission planning, not general exploration.

Vs Quickmap

Quickmap excels at high-res imagery and LOLA topography. LunarLens turns that terrain knowledge into a horizon mask and then computes time-varying Sun and Earth visibility for operational windows.

Vs Moon Trek

Moon Trek gives excellent 3D terrain visualization. LunarLens adds the dynamic sky (Sun/Earth tracks), mission-length scoring, and a landing-date scanner that finds the best dates for power + communication.

Vs Horizons

Horizons gives precise ephemeris. LunarLens uses similar astronomy (astronomy-engine + IAU rotation) and turns the positions into actionable metrics: ops percentage, longest darkness, best landing days, multi-scenario comparison.

Data and resources

Original tools to check this one against and to go deeper on terrain.

JPL Horizons System

NASA JPL ephemeris service. It gives the exact angular positions of the Sun and Earth from any point on the Moon, which is the basis of the numbers here.

ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons

Quickmap

Arizona State University mapping tool for Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data. Use it for imagery and topography of the south pole, for example to read a horizon profile for the mask above.

quickmap.lroc.asu.edu

Moon Trek

NASA's interactive 3D lunar viewer. It shows how terrain around a landing site shapes the horizon and the shadows.

trek.nasa.gov/moon

CSA Moon Exploration

Canadian Space Agency portal on past, current and future lunar missions, Canadian technology and science, and Canada's part in Artemis.

asc-csa.gc.ca/moon-exploration

Method. Sun and Earth directions come from the astronomy-engine ephemeris with the IAU lunar rotation model, so libration and orbital variation are included. Visibility uses the upper limb of the Sun and the centre of Earth, with no atmosphere and no refraction. The Sun counts as hidden while Earth's disc covers its centre. Terrain is not built in: use the horizon mask for local relief. Site coordinates for craters and targets are approximate; use custom coordinates for an exact spot. Checked against Apollo landings: Sun elevation at touchdown comes out at 10.7° for Apollo 11, 5.1° for Apollo 12 and 12.0° for Apollo 15, matching the mission records. Not for flight operations.
